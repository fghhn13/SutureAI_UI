\small
\textbf{What SutureAI does.} Students record a practice task (simple suturing, knot tying or
needle handling) and upload the video. The platform scores it on five dimensions and writes a
report that links each finding to a moment in the video. Instructors follow their whole class,
confirm or adjust scores, and decide students' appeals against individual findings.

\section{How it works}

Every recording goes through the same loop. The student only does two things (upload, then
respond); scoring and the first draft of the report are automatic; the instructor has the final
word on the score and decides any appeal against a finding.

The report scores five \textbf{dimensions}, each from 0 to 100:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabularx}{0.92\linewidth}{@{}>{\bfseries}l X@{}}
\toprule
Efficiency & Duration, path length of the instruments, idle time between steps. \\
Accuracy & Needle entry and exit points, repeated insertion attempts, stitch spacing. \\
Procedural Flow & Whether every step was done, in the right order, without rework. \\
Safety & Dropped needle, wrong instrument, instruments leaving the working area. \\
Product Quality & Consistency of the finished stitches and security of the knots. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\begin{note}
\textbf{Prototype notice.} In this version the video scoring step is a placeholder: scores and
detected events come from a simulation (or, for the demonstration recording, from a manual
annotation) rather than from analysing the video. Everything else (upload, reports, appeals,
reviews, PDF export) works as described.
\end{note}

\section{For students}\label{sec:student}

\subsection{Your dashboard}
The dashboard (\page{/}) is your home page. From top to bottom it shows your course and current
task, any recording that is still being processed, a snapshot of your latest report, your recent
trend, the practice priorities taken from your last report, notifications (for example when your
instructor has reviewed a report or decided one of your appeals), a calendar of training days and your
full history.

\shot{02-student-dashboard.png}{Student dashboard. \ui{Start training} records a new session;
\ui{Open latest report} jumps to your most recent report.}

\subsection{Recording and uploading a session}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.}]
  \item On the dashboard press \ui{Start training} (or \ui{Start this task} on the current-task card).
  \item \textbf{Pick the task} you practised.
  \item \textbf{Add your video}: drag it onto the dashed area or click it to browse. MP4, WebM, MOV and
        MKV are accepted. A preview appears with the file size and duration so you can check it is
        the right recording.
  \item Press \ui{Upload \& analyse}. A progress bar shows the upload; you can cancel it.
\end{enumerate}

\shot[0.86\linewidth]{03-train-upload.png}{Choosing the task and the video before uploading.}

After the upload the page shows the analysis as it happens: the overall percentage with an
estimate of the time left, a checklist of the seven analysis stages (verify the recording, detect
instruments, track hands, segment the procedure steps, score the dimensions, write the report,
link evidence clips) and an \emph{Activity} panel describing what is being done right now. It
usually takes under a minute. When it finishes, the report opens automatically.

You can leave the page at any time: unfinished sessions are listed under \emph{Recordings in
progress} on your dashboard, and the page address (\page{/train/<session>}) can be reopened later.

\shot{04-train-processing.png}{Analysis in progress: stage checklist on the left, live activity on the right.}

\begin{tip}
For the most reliable scores, film from above with the whole practice pad in frame, keep your
hands from covering the needle, use even lighting, and record the task from start to finish
without stopping.
\end{tip}

\subsection{Reading your report}
The top of the report gives the overall score, how confident the system is, the change since your
previous session and the five dimension scores with a radar chart. The badge in the top-right
corner shows whether your instructor has reviewed it yet. If your instructor confirmed a different
score, or accepted one of your appeals, a \emph{Current score} line shows the score that counts.
Use the \ui{Session} menu to switch between your reports.

\shot{05-report-overview.png}{Report header, overall performance and dimension scores.}

The \textbf{timeline} lists what was detected and when: normal steps (blue), warnings (amber) and
issues (red). \ui{Expand} shows the detail; \ui{Replay} (or \ui{Jump}) plays the recording from
that moment. Warnings and issues also have an \ui{Appeal} button (Section~\ref{sec:appeal}).
\emph{Assessment highlights} summarise the strongest dimension, the main issue and what to focus on.

\shot{06-report-timeline.png}{Timeline. The struck-through issue at 01:05 was overturned after the
student's appeal; the warning at 00:47 can still be appealed.}

\shot[0.9\linewidth]{13-report-evidence.png}{Evidence replay. Each key clip's \ui{Replay} button plays
your recording from that moment.}

\subsection{Accepting or appealing your report}\label{sec:appeal}
If you agree with the report, press \ui{Accept report} in the \emph{Your response} box at the bottom.

If you think a finding is wrong, \textbf{appeal it}:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.}]
  \item Find the warning or issue in the timeline and press \ui{Replay} to check the moment yourself.
  \item Press \ui{Appeal} on that event and choose a reason: \emph{This didn't happen},
        \emph{The view was blocked}, \emph{Intentional / wrong moment} or \emph{Other}.
  \item Explain what really happened and press \ui{Send appeal}.
\end{enumerate}

\shot[0.8\linewidth]{14-appeal-form.png}{Appealing a finding directly from the timeline.}

The event then shows \emph{Appeal pending} and your instructor is notified. They watch the clip
and decide:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Accepted}: the finding is struck through as \emph{Overturned after appeal} and your score
        is raised (the badge shows by how much, e.g.\ \emph{+4}).
  \item \textbf{Rejected}: the finding stays; your instructor explains why.
\end{itemize}
Either way the decision and the instructor's reasoning appear under \emph{Your response}. You can
withdraw an appeal while it is still pending, and you can also appeal the report as a whole with
\ui{Appeal the whole report}. A report can carry up to five appeals.

Next to it, \textbf{Instructor evaluation} shows your instructor's final score, comment and teaching
focus once the review is complete, and \textbf{Personal note} is a private reflection saved with the
report; instructors cannot see it.

\shot{07-report-student-feedback.png}{A reviewed report: the appeal on 01:05 was accepted (+2) with the
instructor's reasoning, and the student kept a private note.}

\subsection{Exporting a report as PDF}
Press \ui{Export PDF} at the top of a report. A print layout opens in a new tab and the browser's
print dialog appears; choose \textbf{Save as PDF} as the destination. The PDF contains the scores,
the timeline with a still from the recording at every event, strengths and next steps, all appeals
with their decisions and the instructor's review, so it can be filed as a record of the session.

\shot[0.72\linewidth]{15-export-pdf.png}{First page of an exported report. Each timeline event shows the
video frame at that moment.}

\subsection{Class overview}
Choose a group with the \ui{Cohort} menu; everything on the page refers to that group.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Key numbers}: students (and how many trained this week), sessions this week, the
        30-day average score, reports waiting for your review and open appeals. The last two are
        links into the review queue.
  \item \textbf{Needs attention} lists the students to look at now, most urgent first, each with a
        link to the student or the report.
  \item \textbf{Skill map} shows every student's recent average in each dimension. Paler cells are
        lower scores, so weak spots (for one student, or for the whole group in a column) stand out.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Student detail}
Click a student's name anywhere to open their page: who they are and their current flags, key
numbers, the overall score for every report (hover for details, click a point to open that
report), one small chart per dimension to see \emph{where} a change comes from, every session with
its review and response status, and the training calendar.

\shot{10-instructor-student.png}{A student whose scores are declining. The dimension charts show the
drop is broad rather than in one skill.}

\subsection{Review queue}
The queue collects reports from all your groups (or one, with the \ui{Cohort} filter) in three tabs:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{To review}: reports without a completed review, oldest first. Drafts stay here until completed.
  \item \textbf{Appeals}: reports with at least one appeal waiting for your decision.
  \item \textbf{Reviewed}: completed reviews, most recent first.
\end{itemize}

\shot{11-review-queue.png}{The \emph{Appeals} tab of the review queue. Arrows such as \emph{74 → 72}
show a score you changed in a draft.}

\subsection{Reviewing a report}
Opening a report as an instructor shows the \emph{Report Feedback \& Review} panel at the bottom.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.}]
  \item \textbf{Decide any appeals} under \emph{Student appeals}. Each one shows the disputed finding,
        the student's reason and message; \ui{\faPlay\ 0:03} replays that moment in the recording.
        Choose \textbf{Accept} (the finding is overturned and the score adjusted by the amount you
        set; a value is suggested: +4 for an issue, +2 for a warning) or \textbf{Reject}, explain
        your decision, and press \ui{Send decision}.
  \item Set the \textbf{final score}. It starts at the AI score plus any accepted appeals; the
        \emph{Alignment} box updates as you type.
  \item Write a \textbf{comment to the student} and add one or more \textbf{teaching focus} tags
        (suggestions are offered from the weakest dimensions).
  \item Press \ui{Save draft} to come back later (the student cannot see drafts), or
        \ui{Complete review} to send it. A completed review can still be edited with \ui{Update review}.
\end{enumerate}

\shot{12-review-form.png}{An appeal waiting for a decision, above the review form.}

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabularx}{0.8\linewidth}{@{}l X@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Alignment} & \textbf{Meaning} \\
\midrule
Agrees with AI & Final score equals the AI score. \\
Adjusted & Changed by 10 points or less. \\
Override & Changed by more than 10 points. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

If you accept an appeal after completing the review, the final score is adjusted automatically.

\begin{tip}
The \ui{As student sees it} button at the top of a report previews exactly what the student will
see, which is handy before completing a review.
\end{tip}

\subsection{Troubleshooting}
\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=0pt,font=\bfseries]
  \item[I cannot sign in.]
    Check the email address and password (the password is case-sensitive). If it still fails,
    ask your course administrator to check that your account exists.
  \item[My video is rejected.]
    Only MP4, WebM, MOV and MKV files are accepted, up to 1~GB by default. Empty files are refused.
  \item[Processing failed.]
    Open the session (from \emph{Recordings in progress} on the dashboard) and press \ui{Retry}.
    If it fails again, record a new session with better lighting and framing.
  \item[\ui{Accept report} is greyed out.]
    You have an appeal pending on that report. Wait for the decision or withdraw the appeal first.
  \item[There is no \ui{Appeal} button on an event.]
    Only warnings and issues can be appealed, each once, up to five appeals per report.
  \item[The print dialog did not open after \ui{Export PDF}.]
    Allow pop-ups for the site, or press \ui{Save as PDF} at the top of the print page once the
    video stills have loaded.
  \item[An instructor cannot see a student.]
    Instructors only see students in the groups they teach.
\end{description}
